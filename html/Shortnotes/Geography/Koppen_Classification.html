🔑 2nd Letter — Precipitation / Dryness

German Roots
⚠️ The "What's Missing?" Rule: Lowercase w and s tell you which season is DRY (missing rain), NOT when it rains!

For A, C, D Climates (lowercase):

  • f (feucht = moist) — Full-year rain; no dry season.
  • m — Monsoon (heavy seasonal rain, short dry season).
  • w — Winter dry (so rain is in summer).
  • s — Summer dry (so rain is in winter — classic Mediterranean & Tamil Nadu signature).

For B Climates (CAPITAL):

  • S — Steppe (Semi-arid).
  • W (Wüste = desert) — Wasteland / Desert (Arid).

For E Climates (CAPITAL):

  • T — Tundra (warmest month between 0°C and 10°C).
  • F (Frost) — Frozen solid / Ice Cap (all months below 0°C).

🌡️ 3rd Letter — Temperature Nuances

Lowercase

For B Climates:

  • h (heiß = hot) — Mean annual temp 18°C or higher (low-latitude / subtropical).
  • k (kalt = kold) — Mean annual temp below 18°C (mid-latitude).

For C and D Climates (a → b → c → d gets colder step-by-step):

  • a — Hot summer (warmest month above 22°C).
  • b — Warm summer (warmest month below 22°C; at least 4 months above 10°C).
  • c — Cool, short summer (less than 4 months above 10°C).
  • d — Very severe winter (coldest month below -38°C; Siberia).

India-Specific Suffix:

  • g — Gangetic type: Hottest month occurs before the summer solstice / monsoon onset (i.e., May–June).
Step-down scale: a (hot) → d (Siberia)

🇮🇳 Köppen's 8 Climatic Regions of India (NCERT) — 4-Part Spatial Map Trick

High Yield

1️⃣ Tropic of Cancer Split (~65% of India)

  • Cwg Monsoon with dry winter (Gangetic type)
    North of Tropic of Cancer: Ganga Plains, Eastern Rajasthan, Northern MP, most of North-East India (Assam, etc.).
    Trick: g literally stands for Gangetic.
  • Aw Tropical Savanna
    South of Tropic of Cancer: Most of Peninsular India — includes Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, MP, interior Maharashtra / Telangana.
    Trick: Tropical (A) + winter dry (w).

2️⃣ Two Bottom Coasts (Exact Opposites)

  • Amw Monsoon with short dry winter
    Region: West Coast south of Goa (Malabar & Konkan coast).
    Trick: Malabar Monsoon.
  • As Monsoon with dry summer
    Region: Coromandel Coast of Tamil Nadu (receives winter rain Oct–Dec from retreating NE monsoon).
    Trick: The ONLY `s` (summer dry) in India!

3️⃣ The Dry West

  • BWhw Hot Desert
    Region: Extreme Western Rajasthan (Thar Desert).
    Trick: Wasteland + hot + winter dry.
  • BShw Semi-arid Steppe
    Region: North-western Gujarat, parts of W. Rajasthan, Punjab, and rain-shadow interior of Western Ghats (parts of Karnataka, Maharashtra, Andhra).
    Trick: Semi-arid + hot + winter dry.

4️⃣ The Cold Tops

  • E / ET Polar / Tundra type
    Region: Western / Higher Himalayas — Jammu & Kashmir, Ladakh, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand.
    Trick: Extreme cold / Tundra.
  • Dfc Cold humid winter with short summer
    Region: Arunachal Pradesh (and Sikkim).
    Trick: "Arunachal is Deep-cold, full-year wet, cool short summer."
⚡ Ultimate India Shortcut: Almost every Indian code ends in w (dry winter). Only 3 exceptions break that rule: As (Tamil Nadu), Dfc (Arunachal), and E / ET (Himalayas). Lock those 3 in and the rest fall into place!

🚨 Quick Exam Traps to Watch For

Don't Miss
  • Only As in India has a dry summer: Tamil Nadu's Coromandel Coast is the mirror opposite of the rest of India because it gets rain in Oct–Dec from the retreating North-East Monsoon.
  • Odisha's code is Aw (Tropical Savanna) — NOT Amw or Cwg.
  • Cwg vs Aw boundary: Roughly follows the Tropic of Cancer — north of it is Cwg (Northern Plains), south of it is Aw (Peninsular Plateau).
  • Precipitation-only Group: Among A, B, C, D, E, only B is defined by precipitation (evaporation > rainfall); the other four are temperature-based.

🎓 Scholars of Indian Climatic Regions

OPSC ASO 2021 Q75

Apart from Wladimir Köppen, remember these key scholars who delineated India's climatic regions:

  • Köppen: Empirical; based on mean annual/monthly temperature & precipitation linked to natural vegetation.
  • Thornthwaite: Based on Precipitation Effectiveness (P/E) & Thermal Efficiency (T/E).
  • Trewartha: Modified Köppen classification adapted specifically for India.
  • L.D. Stamp: Divided India into Temperate/Continental & Tropical zones using the 18°C January isotherm.
  • R.L. Singh: Regional geographer who also delineated Indian climatic regions.
