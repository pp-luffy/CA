🌍 International Economic Organizations

Global institutions working in international economic cooperation & stability — UN, World Bank, IMF, WTO, Regional Banks & Trade Blocs

⚡ Ultra-Short Quick Revision Sheet (At-a-Glance)

UNO: 1945 (51 founders) • ECOSOC (simple majority, 1 vote) • UNCTAD (1964, Geneva, meets every 4 yrs).
Bretton Woods: July 1–22, 1944 (New Hampshire, US) • Created IMF & World Bank.
World Bank Group (D.C.): WB = IBRD (1944) + IDA (170+); Group adds IFC (1956), MIGA (1988), ICSID (1966). India NOT in ICSID.
IMF (D.C.): 190 members • Quotas = 25% Reserve/Gold Tranche + 75% Credit Tranche • SDR (1969): USD, EUR, RMB, JPY, GBP.
Regional Banks: ADB (1966, Manila, 67) • AIIB (2015, Beijing; China 26.06%, India 7.5%) • NDB (2015, Shanghai, BRICS, $100B, 1 vote, no veto).
WTO (1995, Geneva): Replaced GATT (1947/48) via 8th Uruguay Round (1986–94) • MFN & National Treatment • TRIMS, TRIPS (7), AOA (3), GATS, SCM.

Americas Blocs: NAFTA (US-Canada-Mexico, >$20T GDP → USMCA) • CAFTA-DR (US + 6) • MERCOSUR (5 S. American nations).

Global Pillar #1

🇺🇳 UNO, ECOSOC & UNCTAD

International Economic Organizations comprise all global institutions working in international economic cooperation and stability (UN, World Bank, IMF, WTO, SAARC, NAFTA, etc.).

United Nations (UNO)

  • Founded in 1945 after the Second World War by 51 countries.
  • Committed to maintaining international peace & security, developing friendly relations among nations, and promoting social progress, better living standards, and human rights.
  • UN Assists In: Peacekeeping • Security • Human Rights • Economic Development • Humanitarian Assistance.

Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC)

  • Principal organ to coordinate the economic, social, and related work of the UN and its specialized agencies & institutions.
  • Voting is by simple majority; each member has one vote.

UNCTAD (UN Conference on Trade & Development)

  • Principal organ of UNGA dealing with trade, investment, and development issues.
  • Established in 1964; permanent secretariat in Geneva.
  • Primary Objective: Formulate policies relating to all aspects of development — trade, aid, transport, finance, and technology.
  • Ordinarily meets once in four years.
UN System • Peace & Development
Bretton Woods Twin #1

🏛️ Bretton Woods & World Bank Group

Bretton Woods Conference: Site of the first world economic conference — UN Monetary and Financial Conference held in Bretton Woods, New Hampshire, US from July 1 to July 22, 1944. Created both the IMF and World Bank. The Bretton Woods Agreement (1944) is the landmark system for monetary & exchange rate management.

World Bank vs. World Bank Group

  • Part of the UN system, but its governance structure is different. HQ: Washington, D.C.
  • World Bank (2 institutions): IBRD + IDA.
  • World Bank Group (5 institutions): IBRD + IDA + IFC + MIGA + ICSID.
  • India's Status: Founder member of IBRD, IDA, and IFC. However, India is NOT a member of ICSID.

The 5 Institutions

  • IBRD (1944): International Bank for Reconstruction and Development. Created to help Europe rebuild after WWII; joins with IDA to form World Bank; works with public & private sectors in developing countries to reduce poverty & build shared prosperity.
  • IDA: International Development Association. Helps world's poorest countries. Overseen by 170+ shareholder nations. Provides loans (called "credits") and grants to boost growth, reduce inequalities, and improve living conditions. Complements IBRD.
  • IFC (1956): International Finance Corporation. Supports private sector growth in developing world. Mission: promote sustainable private sector investment. Provides loans, equity, advice, and technical services to private sector (while WB assists governments).
  • MIGA (1988): Multilateral Investment Guarantee Agency. Investment insurance facility promoting cross-border investment in developing countries via guarantees (political risk insurance & credit enhancement) to investors & lenders.
  • ICSID (1966): International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes. Established by the ICSID Convention (between States and Nationals of Other States). Leading forum for investor-State dispute settlement in treaties, laws, and contracts.
HQ: Washington, D.C. • Development Arm
Bretton Woods Twin #2

💰 International Monetary Fund (IMF)

WB vs. IMF Key Distinction: World Bank is not a bank in the conventional sense (performs development function), whereas IMF performs the lending function associated with banks.
  • Members: 190 countries.
  • Headquarters: Washington, D.C. (offices in Paris, Tokyo, New York, and Geneva).
  • Functions: Lending institution giving advances to members in need; mentor of members' monetary & exchange rate policies; maintains stability in global exchange rate system.

Lending Facilities

  • Extended Fund Facility (EFF — 1974): Helps countries address medium- and longer-term Balance of Payments (BoP) problems caused by structural weaknesses requiring fundamental economic reforms.
  • Poverty Reduction & Growth Facility (PRGF — Sept 1999): Replaced the Enhanced Structural Adjustment Facility; lends to the world's poorest countries.

Quotas & Tranches

  • Every member subscribes a Quota reflecting its relative position in the world economy, denominated in SDRs (IMF's unit of account).
  • 25% Reserve / Gold Tranche: Paid in foreign currency or gold.
  • 75% Credit Tranche: Paid in domestic currency.

Special Drawing Rights (SDR)

  • International reserve asset / reserve money used alongside gold & US dollars.
  • Established in 1969 to alleviate the problem of international liquidity.
  • 5-Currency Basket: US Dollar, Euro, Chinese Renminbi, Japanese Yen, British Pound Sterling.
  • Allocated in proportion to quota subscriptions. Only IMF members can participate.
Monetary Stability & BoP Lending
Multilateral Banks

🏦 ADB, AIIB & NDB

Asian Development Bank (ADB)

  • Established 19 December 1966; HQ: Manila, Philippines (+ field offices worldwide).
  • Promotes social & economic development in Asia via loans, technical assistance, grants, and equity investments.
  • Members: Grew from 31 at establishment to 67 members (48 Asia-Pacific + 19 outside).
  • Modelled closely on World Bank; weighted voting system proportional to capital subscriptions.

Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB)

  • Initiated by China; finances infrastructure development & regional connectivity in Asia-Pacific.
  • Viewed as Asia's response to West-dominated ADB and World Bank.
  • Officially established Dec 2015; opened for business Jan 2016; HQ: Beijing, China.
  • Shareholding: China = largest (26.06% voting shares); India = 2nd largest (7.5% vote share).

New Development Bank (NDB)

  • Promoted by BRICS (Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa); outcome of 6th BRICS Summit (Fortaleza, Brazil).
  • HQ: Shanghai, China; regional office in Johannesburg, South Africa.
  • Began operations July 2015 with initial capital of $100 billion.
  • Funds infrastructure projects in emerging economies for sustainable development.
  • Governance: Each participant country has one vote; no veto power.

Global Trade Rules

⚖️ WTO, GATT & Core Principles

  • WTO: Established 1995; HQ: Geneva, Switzerland. Operates global trade rules, settles trade disputes, and provides a forum where negotiations are conducted directly & exclusively by Member governments. Agreements are legally binding contracts.
  • GATT (General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade): Signed by 23 nations in Geneva on 30 Oct 1947; took effect 1 Jan 1948. Aimed to reduce/eliminate tariffs & quotas.
  • Uruguay Round (1986–1994): 8th round of GATT negotiations → led to formation of WTO.
  • India: Member of GATT since 1948 → party to Uruguay Round & founding member of WTO.
  • Scope Shift: GATT applied only to goods; WTO covers goods, services, and trade-related IPR.

Principle of Non-Discrimination

Applies to trade in goods, services, and TRIPS:
  • Most-Favoured Nation (MFN): An advantage granted to one country must be given to all WTO Members.
  • National Treatment: Must not discriminate between imports and like domestic products.
Rules-Based Multilateral Trade
WTO Legal Pillars

📜 Major WTO Agreements

  • TRIMS (Trade-Related Investment Measures): Prohibits investment measures that restrict/distort trade, discriminate against foreign products, or create quantitative restrictions (e.g., local content requirements).
  • TRIPS (Trade-Related Intellectual Property Rights): IPR = "Information with commercial value." Sets minimum protection & enforcement standards across 7 areas:
    (a) Patents • (b) Copyright • (c) Industrial design • (d) Geographical indication • (e) Trademarks • (f) Trade secrets • (g) Layout design (topographies of integrated circuits).
  • Agreement on Agriculture (AOA — 1995): Reforms agricultural trade to be fairer & more competitive (some negotiations ongoing). Stands on 3 Pillars: Domestic Support, Market Access, Export Subsidies.
  • GATS (1995): General Agreement on Trade in Services — first multilateral agreement covering trade in services (negotiated in Uruguay Round).

SCM Agreement & Trade Duties

  • Subsidies & Countervailing Measures (SCM): Regulates subsidies (financial contribution by/at direction of government/public body conferring benefit to industry) and countervailing measures.
  • Actionable Subsidies: Not prohibited, but can face countervailing measures or be challenged in WTO's Dispute Resolution Body. Duties must be transparent with a specified sunset period.
  • Countervailing Duty: Imposed on imports to counterbalance a subsidy provided by the exporter country.
  • Anti-Dumping Duty: Counterbalances heavy subsidization where exporters sell goods below domestic price or cost of production in foreign markets to wipe out the target country's industry.

South Asia

🌏 SAARC & SAFTA

SAARC (South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation)

  • Regional intergovernmental organization & geopolitical union of South Asia.
  • Share (as of 2015): 3% world area • 21% world population • 3.8% global economy.
  • Founded in Dhaka on 8 December 1985; Secretariat in Kathmandu, Nepal.
  • Promotes economic & regional integration; launched SAFTA in 2006.
  • Holds permanent diplomatic relations at UN as an observer; links with EU & multilateral entities.

SAFTA (South Asian Free Trade Area)

  • Agreement reached on January 6, 2004 at the 12th SAARC Summit in Islamabad, Pakistan.
  • 8 Members: Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Maldives, Nepal, Pakistan, Sri Lanka.
  • Created a free trade area of 1.6 billion people (combined population 1.8 billion as of 2011).
  • Framework signed by foreign ministers to reduce customs duties of all traded goods in a phased manner.

The Americas

🌎 NAFTA, CAFTA-DR & MERCOSUR

NAFTA (North American Free Trade Agreement)

  • Trilateral rules-based trading bloc: Canada, Mexico, United States.
  • World's largest FTA; combined GDP more than $20 trillion.
  • First time two developed nations signed a trade agreement with an emerging market country.
  • Now replaced by USMCA.

CR–CAFTA / CAFTA-DR

  • Central America and Dominican Republic Free Trade Agreement.
  • First FTA between the United States and a group of smaller developing economies: Costa Rica, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, Nicaragua, and Dominican Republic.
  • Promotes stronger trade & investment ties, prosperity, and stability across the region and along the US Southern border.

MERCOSUR

  • Economic & political bloc: Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay, Uruguay, Venezuela.
  • Created when longtime rivals Argentina & Brazil sought to improve relations; saw a tenfold increase in intra-group trade in the 1990s.
North, Central & South America
Europe

🇪🇺 European Union, Euro & Brexit

  • Political & economic union of 27 member states located primarily in Europe.
  • Internal Single Market: Standardised laws ensuring free movement of people, goods, services, and capital; legislation in justice & home affairs; common policies on trade, agriculture, fisheries, and regional development.
  • Schengen Area: Passport controls have been abolished for travel within the area.
  • Monetary Union & Euro: Established in 1999, full force in 2002; composed of 19 EU member states using the Euro (official common currency of the Eurozone).
  • Brexit: Shorthand for Britain + exit (UK leaving the EU; noted in text as due on 29 March, 2019).
Single Market & Monetary Union
Asia-Pacific

🌴 ASEAN, PACER Plus & RCEP

ASEAN (Association of Southeast Asian Nations)

  • Regional grouping promoting economic, political, and security cooperation among 10 members: Brunei, Cambodia, Indonesia, Laos, Malaysia, Myanmar, Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, Vietnam.
  • Population: nearly 640 million; combined GDP: $2.57 trillion.
  • Signed six FTAs with other regional economies.

PACER Plus

  • Pacific Agreement on Closer Economic Relations Plus (negotiations since 2009).
  • PACER: Umbrella agreement between Pacific Islands Forum members (Forum Island Countries + Australia & New Zealand).
  • First signed at Nauru on 18 August 2001; entered into force 3 October 2002. Sets framework for future trade & economic relations.

RCEP (Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership)

  • Negotiated since 2013.
  • Proposed FTA between the 10 ASEAN member states + 6 Asia-Pacific states with existing ASEAN FTAs: Australia, China, India, Japan, South Korea, New Zealand.
  • Source notes a broad agreement is likely to be reached soon.
