🦋 Core Biodiversity Conventions & Global Frameworks

CBD & Its Protocols (Rio Convention)

  • Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD) 1992 / 1993: 3 goals — conservation, sustainable use, and fair & equitable benefit-sharing.
  • Cartagena Protocol 2000 / 2003: Biosafety protocol under CBD; regulates safe handling/transport of Living Modified Organisms (LMOs) via Advance Informed Agreement (AIA).
  • Nagoya Protocol 2010 / 2014: Access & Benefit Sharing (ABS) of genetic resources.
  • Nagoya–Kuala Lumpur Supplementary Protocol 2010 / 2018: Liability and redress for damage caused by LMOs (supplementary to Cartagena).
  • Kunming–Montreal GBF (COP 15) 2022: Replaced Aichi Targets; famous for the "30×30" target (protect 30% of land & oceans by 2030).

CITES, CMS & Ramsar Conventions

  • CITES (Washington Convention) 1973 / 1975: Regulates international trade in endangered wild fauna & flora; legally binding; 3 Appendices (I, II, III).
  • CMS (Bonn Convention) 1979 / 1983: Conservation of Migratory Species of Wild Animals (Appendices I & II). India hosted COP 13 (Gandhinagar, 2020).
  • Ramsar Convention 1971 / 1975: Conservation and wise use of Wetlands. Maintains the Montreux Record (register of wetlands facing ecological change).
  • World Heritage Convention (WHC) 1972 / 1975: UNESCO treaty protecting Cultural, Natural & Mixed sites of Outstanding Universal Value.

Plants, Forests, MAB & Hotspots

  • ITPGRFA (Seed Treaty) 2001 / 2004: FAO treaty on Plant Genetic Resources for Food & Agriculture; ensures farmers' rights & seed exchange.
  • IPPC (Plant Protection) 1951 / 1952: Prevents introduction and spread of pests affecting plants & plant products.
  • ITTA (Tropical Timber) 1983 / rev. 2006: Promotes sustainable management & legal trade of tropical timber (administered by ITTO).
  • Bonn Challenge 2011: Restore 150M ha of degraded land by 2020 & 350M ha by 2030 (India pledged 26M ha by 2030).
  • UNESCO MAB Programme 1971: Man and the Biosphere — intergovernmental programme behind World Network of Biosphere Reserves.
  • Biodiversity Hotspots 1988, Norman Myers: 36 globally; 4 in India: Himalayas, Indo-Burma, Sundaland (Nicobar), Western Ghats & Sri Lanka.
🌊 Marine Treaties, Ozone Depletion & Hazardous Chemicals

Marine, Polar & Environmental Rights

  • BBNJ Agreement (High Seas Treaty) 2023 / Jan 2026: First legally binding treaty under UNCLOS for marine biodiversity in Areas Beyond National Jurisdiction (in force 17 Jan 2026; India signed, ratification pending).
  • ICRW (Whaling Convention) 1946 / 1948: Created the International Whaling Commission (IWC) to regulate whale conservation & whaling.
  • MARPOL (73/78) 1973 / 1978: Main international convention preventing marine pollution from ships (oil, chemicals, garbage).
  • London Convention 1972: Prevents marine pollution by regulating the dumping of wastes at sea.
  • Antarctic Treaty & Madrid Protocol 1959 / 1991: Designates Antarctica as a natural reserve for peace & science; bans mining.
  • Aarhus Convention 1998: Grants public rights to environmental information, participation, and justice.

☀️ Ozone Layer Depletion (Complete Pack)

  • Vienna Convention 1985 / 1988: Framework for protecting the Ozone Layer.
  • Montreal Protocol 1987: Phases out Ozone Depleting Substances (ODS) — CFCs, Halons, HCFCs, Carbon Tetrachloride, Methyl Bromide.
  • Kigali Amendment 2016: Phases down HFCs.
    ⚠️ Exam Trap: HFCs do NOT deplete ozone; they are potent GHGs!
  • World Ozone Day: 16 September (signing of Montreal Protocol, 16 Sept 1987).
  • Dobson Unit (DU): Measures ozone thickness; below 220 DU = "Ozone Hole".
  • Good vs. Bad Ozone: Stratospheric = Good (blocks UV-B); Tropospheric = Bad (secondary air pollutant & GHG).
  • Polar Stratospheric Clouds (PSCs): Ice clouds over Antarctica accelerating chlorine reactions → Antarctic Ozone Hole in spring (Sept–Oct).

☣️ Hazardous Waste & Chemical Treaties

  • Basel Convention 1989 / 1992: Controls transboundary movement and disposal of Hazardous Wastes.
  • Rotterdam Convention 1998 / 2004: Prior Informed Consent (PIC) procedure for hazardous chemicals and pesticides in international trade.
  • Stockholm Convention 2001 / 2004: Eliminates or restricts Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs) (initially the "Dirty Dozen").
  • Minamata Convention 2013 / 2017: Protects human health and the environment from Mercury emissions and releases.
🏛️ Key Global Biodiversity Organisations & Alliances

IUCN, TRAFFIC & IPBES

  • IUCN: Publishes the Red List of Threatened Species (9 categories from Not Evaluated to Extinct).
  • TRAFFIC: Joint wildlife trade monitoring network of WWF and IUCN.
  • IPBES: Intergovernmental Science-Policy Platform on Biodiversity & Ecosystem Services (often called the "IPCC for biodiversity").

GTF, IBCA & Bird/Wetland NGOs

  • Global Tiger Forum (GTF): Only intergovernmental body solely dedicated to wild tiger conservation (HQ: New Delhi).
  • International Big Cat Alliance (IBCA): India-led coalition for 7 big cats — Tiger, Lion, Leopard, Snow Leopard, Puma, Jaguar, Cheetah.
  • BirdLife International & Wetlands International: Key NGOs designating Important Bird Areas (IBAs) and advising Ramsar.
