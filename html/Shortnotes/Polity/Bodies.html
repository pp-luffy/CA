Indian Polity: Constitutional & Statutory Bodies

Comprehensive Master Sketchnote Visual Board & Quick Revision Sheet

⚡ Ultra-Short Quick Revision Sheet (Core Facts)

ECI / UPSC / CAG: Tenure 6 yrs / 65 yrs age.
SPSC Tenure: 6 yrs / 62 yrs age (Appointed by Governor, Removed by President).
NCSC / NCST / NCBC: 3-year tenure, appointed by the President.
AGI / Adv. General: Hold office during the pleasure of President / Governor. No fixed term.
CVC / Lokpal: CVC (4 yrs / 65 yrs), Lokpal (5 yrs / 70 yrs).
SHRC Tenure: 3 yrs / 70 yrs (Amended in 2019 from 5 yrs).
CBI: Non-statutory; powers derived from DSPE Act, 1946.
NCBC Amendment: Elevated to Constitutional Body via 102nd Amendment Act, 2018.
Part 1: Constitutional Bodies
1. Election Commission of India Art 324
  • Part: XV
  • Composition: CEC & other ECs fixed by President (Multi-member body).
  • Appointment: Appointed by the President.
  • Tenure: 6 years or 65 years of age (whichever is earlier).
  • Removal:
    • CEC: Same as SC Judge (Special Majority).
    • Other ECs: President on CEC's recommendation.
💡 Key Point: Not barred from further appointment by the government.
2. Union Public Service Commission Art 315-323
  • Part: XIV
  • Composition: Chairman & 9-11 members.
  • Appointment: Appointed by the President.
  • Tenure: 6 years or 65 years of age.
  • Removal: By President on misbehavior grounds after Supreme Court inquiry.
💡 Key Point: Chairman is ineligible for further employment under Govt of India or State.
3. State Public Service Commission Art 315-323
  • Part: XIV
  • Composition: Chairman & members.
  • Appointment: Appointed by the Governor.
  • Tenure: 6 years or 62 years of age.
  • Removal: Appointed by Governor, but removed only by the President (via SC inquiry).
💡 Key Point: Removal process mirrors UPSC via Supreme Court inquiry report.
4. Finance Commission Art 280
  • Part: XII
  • Composition: Chairman and 4 other members.
  • Appointment: Appointed by the President.
  • Tenure: Specified by presidential order; eligible for reappointment.
  • Removal: As per terms set by presidential order.
💡 Key Point: Quasi-judicial body recommending tax revenue distribution between Centre and States.
5. Comptroller & Auditor General Art 148
  • Part: V
  • Appointment: President (by warrant under hand and seal).
  • Tenure: 6 years or 65 years of age.
  • Removal: Same as Supreme Court Judge (Special Majority in Parliament).
💡 Key Point: Ineligible for further office post-retirement. Described by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar as the most important officer.
6. Nat. Comm. for Scheduled Castes Art 338
  • Part: XVI
  • Composition: Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson & 3 members.
  • Appointment: Appointed by the President.
  • Tenure: 3 years (determined by President).
  • Removal: Pleasure of the President / presidential orders.
💡 Key Point: Constitutional status via 65th Amendment 1990; split into NCSC & NCST via 89th Amendment 2003.
7. Nat. Comm. for Scheduled Tribes Art 338-A
  • Part: XVI
  • Composition: Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson & 3 members.
  • Appointment: Appointed by the President.
  • Tenure: 3 years.
  • Removal: Presidential orders.
💡 Key Point: Established separately by the 89th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003.
8. Nat. Comm. for Backward Classes Art 338-B
  • Part: XVI
  • Composition: Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson & 3 members.
  • Appointment: Appointed by the President.
  • Tenure: 3 years.
  • Removal: Presidential orders.
💡 Key Point: Elevated from statutory (1993 Act) to Constitutional Body via landmark 102nd Amendment Act, 2018.
9. Attorney General of India Art 76
  • Part: V
  • Appointment: Appointed by President. Must be qualified to be an SC Judge.
  • Tenure: Not fixed by the Constitution.
  • Removal: Holds office during the pleasure of the President.
💡 Key Point: Highest law officer; can speak & take part in Parliamentary proceedings, but cannot vote.
10. Advocate General of the State Art 165
  • Part: VI
  • Appointment: Appointed by Governor. Must be qualified to be a HC Judge.
  • Tenure: Not fixed by the Constitution.
  • Removal: Holds office during the pleasure of the Governor.
💡 Key Point: State-level counterpart to the Attorney General of India.
Part 2: Non-Constitutional (Statutory & Executive) Bodies
1. Central Vigilance Commission Statutory
  • Law / Year: Exec. resolution 1964; Statutory via CVC Act, 2003.
  • Composition: Central Vigilance Commissioner & max 2 Vigilance Commissioners.
  • Appointment: President on recommendation of 3-member committee (PM, Home Minister, LoP Lok Sabha).
  • Tenure: 4 years or 65 years of age.
  • Removal: By President on misbehavior after Supreme Court inquiry.
💡 Key Point: Apex anti-corruption body monitoring vigilance administration in Central govt.
2. Central Bureau of Investigation Executive
  • Law / Year: Home Ministry resolution 1963; powers from DSPE Act, 1946.
  • Appointment: Headed by Director appointed by committee (PM, LoP Lok Sabha, CJI / SC Judge).
  • Tenure: Fixed tenure of 2 years (extendable up to 5 years).
💡 Key Point: CBI is not a statutory body by a standalone "CBI Act"; derives powers from DSPE Act.
3. Lokpal and Lokayuktas Statutory
  • Law / Year: Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013.
  • Composition: Chairperson (former CJI/SC judge/eminent person) + max 8 members (50% judicial).
  • Appointment: President on recommendation of high-powered Selection Committee (PM, Speaker, LoP, CJI, Jurist).
  • Tenure: 5 years or 70 years of age.
  • Removal: President based on SC inquiry reference (petition signed by 100+ MPs).
💡 Key Point: Anti-corruption ombudsman at the national level.
4. State Human Rights Commissions Statutory
  • Law / Year: Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993 (amended in 2019).
  • Composition: Chairperson (former HC Chief Justice/Judge) & members.
  • Appointment: Governor on recommendation of committee (CM, Home Minister, Speaker, LoP).
  • Tenure: 3 years or 70 years of age (amended from 5 yrs in 2019).
  • Removal: Appointed by Governor, but removed only by the President (via SC inquiry).
💡 Key Point: Aligned with NHRC framework for investigating human rights violations at state level.
