Overview

General Characteristics

  • Most hills are relict type (residual hills).
  • They are remnants of hills and horsts formed millions of years ago.
  • Horst: uplifted block. Graben: subsided block.
  • These hill ranges and river valleys separate the plateaus of the Peninsular region.
Aravali Range

The Aravali Range

  • Alignment: NE to SW direction.
  • Extent: ~800 km between Delhi and Palanpur (Gujarat).
  • Age & Type: Oldest fold mountains in India (and world). Formed in Archaean Era. Now highly weathered relict mountains.
  • Topography: Continuous range south of Ajmer (rises to 900m). Discontinuous/detached ridges in Haryana & Delhi. Buried under Ganga alluvium up to Haridwar.
  • Extensions (Theories): To Lakshadweep via Gulf of Khambhat, and into AP/Karnataka.
  • Elevation: Generally 400-600m. SW extremity rises >1,000m.
  • Peaks & Passes: Guru Sikhar (1,722m) in Mt. Abu (separated by Banas valley). Passes: Pipli Ghat, Dewair, Desuri.
Vindhyan Range

The Vindhyan Range

  • Location: North of Narmada Valley. Forms an escarpment flanking the Narmada-Son Trough.
  • Extent: East-west direction from Jobat (Gujarat) to Sasaram (Bihar) for >1,200 km.
  • Elevation: 300 to 650 m.
  • Geology: Composed of horizontally bedded ancient sedimentary rocks.
  • Eastern Extensions: Bharner and Kaimur hills.
  • Significance: Acts as a watershed between the Ganga system and South Indian river systems. (Rivers Chambal, Betwa, Ken rise within 30km of Narmada).
Satpura Range

The Satpura Range

  • Name: 'Sat' = seven, 'pura' = mountains. Series of 7 mountains.
  • Location: Runs E-W, south of Vindhyas. Lies between Narmada and Tapi rivers.
  • Extent: ~900 km.
  • Structure: Parts are folded and upheaved. Regarded as a structural uplift or 'horst'.
  • Key Peaks: Dhupgarh (1,350m) near Pachmarhi on Mahadev Hills (Highest). Amarkantak (1,127m).
Western Ghats

Western Ghats (Sahyadris)

  • Form the western edge of Deccan tableland.
  • Extent: Tapi valley (21°N) to north of Kanniyakumari (11°N). Length: 1,600 km.
  • Topography: Steep-sided, terraced, flat-topped hills. 'Landing stair aspect' due to weathering of horizontal lavas.
  • Elevation: Rises as a sheer wall to avg 1,000m from the coast. Gentle eastern slope.
North Sahyadri

Northern Section

  • Tapi valley to north of Goa.
  • Geology: Horizontal sheets of Deccan lavas (Deccan Traps).
  • Average Height: 1,200 m.
  • Peaks: Kalasubai (1,646m), Salher (1,567m), Mahabaleshwar (1,438m), Harishchandragarh (1,424m).
  • Passes: Thal ghat & Bhor ghat (Connect Konkan Plains to Deccan Plateau).
Middle Sahyadri

Middle Section

  • 16°N latitude to Nilgiri hills.
  • Geology: Granites and gneisses.
  • Features: Dense forests, western scarp dissected by headward erosion. Avg height 1200m.
  • Peaks: Vavul Mala (2,339m), Kudremukh (1,892m), Pashpagiri (1,714m).
  • Nilgiri Hills: Trijunction of KA, KL, TN. Rises >2,000m. Junction of Western & Eastern Ghats. Peaks: Doda Betta (2,637m), Makurti (2,554m).
South Sahyadri

Southern Section

  • Separated from main range by Pal ghat Gap (Palakkad Gap), a rift valley connecting TN and KL. Allows SW monsoon to enter Mysore region.
  • Topography: Intricate steep/rugged slopes.
  • Highest Peak: Anai Mudi (2,695m) - Highest in South India.
  • Radiating Ranges from Anai Mudi:
    - North: Anaimalai (1800-2000m)
    - North-East: Palani (900-1,200m)
    - South: Cardamom Hills / Ealaimalai
Eastern Ghats

The Eastern Ghats

  • Parallel to east coast. Extent: Mahanadi (Odisha) to Vagai (TN). Almost disappear between Godavari & Krishna.
  • Structure: Highly broken, detached hills. No structural unity or continuity.
  • Northern Part (Mahanadi to Godavari): True mountain character.
    - Maliya Range: 900-1200m. Peak: Mahendra Giri (1,501m).
    - Madugula Konda Range: 1100-1400m. Peaks: Jindhagada (1,690m) in Araku Valley, Arma Konda (1,680m), Gali Konda, Sinkram Gutta.
  • Godavari-Krishna gap: Occupied by Gondwana formations (KG Basin).
  • Andhra Pradesh: Reappears in Cuddapah/Kurnool as Nallamalai Range (600-850m). Southern part = Palkonda range.
  • Southern Hills: Low altitudes. Distinct hills @ 1,000m: Javadi Hills, Shevroy-Kalrayan Hills.
  • Biligiri Rangan Hills (KA/TN border): 1,279m. Merges with Western Ghats further south.
Significance

Economic & Geographic Importance

  • Minerals: Huge deposits of iron, manganese, copper, bauxite, chromium, mica, gold.
  • Coal: 98% of India's Gondwana coal is here.
  • Building Materials: Slate, shale, sandstone, marble.
  • Agriculture:
    - NW plateau: Fertile black lava soil (Cotton).
    - South Hills: Plantation crops (Tea, coffee, rubber).
    - Low-lying areas: Rice.
  • Forests: Highland forests provide diverse products.
  • Hydropower & Irrigation: Rivers from Western Ghats.
  • Tourism: Hill resorts like Ooty, Panchmarhi, Kodaikanal, Mahabaleshwar, Khandala, Mount Abu.
