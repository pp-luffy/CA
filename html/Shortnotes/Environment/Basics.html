🤝 7 Biotic (Population) Interactions & Every Exam Example

1. Commensalism (+, 0)

One species benefits; the other is unaffected (neither harmed nor benefited).

  • Barnacle attached to a Whale (gets mobility & food access; whale unaffected).
  • Suckerfish (Remora) attached to a Shark (hitches a ride & feeds on scraps).
  • Epiphytes & Trees (e.g., orchids growing on tree trunks purely for structural support & sunlight, no nutrient theft).

2. Amensalism (-, 0)

One species is harmed/inhibited; the other is unaffected (often via chemical inhibition / Allelopathy).

  • Small plant growing under a Big Tree (shaded out from sunlight/nutrients).
  • Black Walnut & other plants (secretes toxic juglone inhibiting nearby growth).
  • Antibiotic-producing & non-producing Bacteria (e.g., Penicillium inhibits bacteria).
  • Sunflower & nearby plants (allelopathic inhibition).
  • Eucalyptus & nearby plants (chemicals & heavy water draw suppress nearby flora).

3. Protocooperation (+, +) Optional

Both benefit, but the interaction is non-obligatory (facultative) — both can survive independently.

  • Ants & Aphids (ants protect aphids & feed on honeydew).
  • Sea Anemone & Hermit Crab (anemone gets mobility; crab gets stinging protection).
  • Pollination (insects/birds get nectar; plants get cross-pollinated).
  • Birds & Grazing Animals (birds eat small insects/ticks off grazers; grazers get relief).
  • Lion & Hyena (sometimes becomes protocooperation when sharing/flushing prey or scavenging cues).

4. Symbiosis (Obligate Mutualism) (+, +) Strict

Both benefit and are strictly dependent on each other for survival.

  • Coral Reef (Coral polyps + Zooxanthellae algae — algae photosynthesise food, polyps provide shelter; breakdown causes coral bleaching).
  • Lichen (Algae + Fungi — algae make food, fungi provide structure/moisture; also a classic SO₂ pollution indicator).

5. Competition (-, -)

Both species/organisms are negatively affected while vying for the same limited resource (food, light, nesting site, mate).

  • Interspecific Competition: Between different species (e.g., two bird species competing for the exact same nesting sites).
  • Intraspecific Competition: Within the same species (usually more intense because resource needs are identical).
  • Gause's Principle: Two species cannot occupy the exact same ecological niche indefinitely.

6. Predation & 7. Parasitism (+, -)

One organism benefits (+) while the other is harmed (-):

  • Predation: Predator hunts, kills, and consumes prey immediately.
    → Example: Lion & Goat (controls prey population & transfers energy up food chain).
  • Parasitism: Parasite lives on/inside a host and feeds on it without immediately killing it.
    → Example: Leech on a person or animal (ectoparasite feeding on blood).
🌍 Core Ecology Fundamentals (High-Yield Exam Concepts)

In-situ vs. Ex-situ Conservation

  • In-situ (On-site / Natural Habitat): Protects species within their native ecosystem.
    • Examples: National Parks, Wildlife Sanctuaries, Biosphere Reserves, Tiger Reserves, Conservation & Community Reserves, Sacred Groves.
  • Ex-situ (Off-site / Artificial Setup): Protects threatened species outside their natural habitat.
    • Examples: Zoos (Zoological Parks), Botanical Gardens, Seed Banks, Gene Banks, Cryopreservation (liquid nitrogen at -196°C), Tissue Culture.

5 Ecological Species Roles

  • Keystone Species: Disproportionately huge impact on ecosystem stability relative to its small population (e.g., Tiger, Sea Otter, Bees, Figs).
  • Indicator Species: Sensitive to environmental changes; acts as an early warning (e.g., Lichens for SO₂ pollution; Amphibians/Frogs for water/habitat health).
  • Flagship Species: Charismatic ambassador species chosen to rally public conservation support (e.g., Bengal Tiger, Giant Panda, Indian Elephant).
  • Umbrella Species: Requires a huge habitat; protecting it automatically protects many co-existing species (e.g., Tiger, Spotted Owl).
  • Invasive (Alien) Species: Non-native species that spreads aggressively and threatens natives (e.g., Lantana camara, Water Hyacinth, Prosopis juliflora).

Energy Flow & Ecological Pyramids

Apex Predator (10 J)
Secondary Consumer (100 J)
Primary Consumer (1,000 J)
Producers / Autotrophs (10,000 J)
  • 10% Law (Raymond Lindeman): Only 10% of energy transfers to the next trophic level; 90% is lost as heat. Flow is always unidirectional.
  • Pyramid of Energy: ALWAYS Upright (never inverted).
  • Pyramid of Biomass: Upright on land; Inverted in Aquatic/Marine ecosystems (phytoplankton biomass < fish biomass).
  • Pyramid of Number: Upright in grasslands; Inverted in a single-tree / parasitic food chain.

Ecotone, Edge Effect & Succession

  • Ecotone: Zone of junction/transition between two biomes (e.g., Mangroves between marine & terrestrial; Estuaries; Grasslands).
  • Edge Effect: Tendency for greater variety & density of species in the ecotone than in either adjoining community. Species thriving mainly here = Edge Species.
  • Primary Succession: Starts on lifeless bare rock/lava (no soil). First colonisers = Pioneer Species (Lichens & Mosses). Very slow.
  • Secondary Succession: Occurs after disturbance (fire, flood) where soil already exists. Much faster than primary succession.
  • Climax Community: Final, stable, self-perpetuating stage of succession.
  • Ecological Niche: Unique functional role + physical habitat of a species in an ecosystem.
