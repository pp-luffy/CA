🏞️ Peninsular Plateau

⚡ Quick Revision Core Facts

Area: 16 lakh sq km (India is 32)
Elevation: 600-900 m Avg
Shape: Triangular (Apex: Kanniyakumari)
Rocks: Archaean gneisses, schists
Slope: West to East
Exception: Narmada-Tapti flow W in Rift
Highest Peak (Meghalaya): Shillong (1,961 m)
Highest Peak (Karnataka): Mulangiri (1,913 m)
Coal Hub: Gondwana rocks (Chotanagpur)
Black Soil: Malwa & Maharashtra (Lava origin)
General

Key Features

  • Base coincides with North India Plain, Apex at Kanniyakumari.
  • One of the oldest landforms, highly stable shield block.
  • Little structural change, never submerged (mostly).
  • Aggregation of smaller plateaus, hill ranges, river basins & valleys.
Rajasthan

Marwar / Mewar Plateau

  • Located in eastern Rajasthan (East of Aravalis).
  • Elevation: 250-500 m; Slopes eastwards.
  • Rocks: Sandstone, shales, limestones of Vindhayan period.
  • Rivers: Banas + tributaries (Berach, Khari) flow NW to Chambal.
  • Appears as a Rolling Plain due to erosional activity.
Central

Central Highland

  • AKA Madhya Bharat Pathar (East of Marwar).
  • Comprises basin of Chambal river (flows in rift valley).
  • Tributaries: Kali Sindh, Banas, Parwan, Parbati.
  • Features: Rolling plateau, rounded sandstone hills, thick forests.
  • North marked by Chambal Ravines (Badlands).
UP / MP

Bundelkhand Upland

  • Borders: Yamuna (N), Madhya Bharat (W), Vindhyan Scarp (E/SE), Malwa (S).
  • Made of 'Bundelkhand gneiss' (granite/gneiss).
  • Elevation: 300-600 m; Slopes down to Yamuna.
  • Spread over 5 UP dists & 4 MP dists.
  • Characterized by Senile topography, undulating, unfit for cultivation.
  • Rivers: Betwa, Dhasan, Ken.
Central

Malwa Plateau

  • Triangle: Vindhyan Hills (base), Aravali (W), Madhya Bharat (N), Bundelkhand (E).
  • 2 Drainages: Arabian Sea (Narmada, Tapi, Mahi) & Bay of Bengal (Chambal, Betwa).
  • North drained by Chambal, Kali, Sindh, Parbati, Ken, Betwa.
  • Composed of extensive lava flow & covered in Black Soils.
  • Slope towards North (600m to <500m); Chambal ravines in North.
East-Central

Baghelkhand

  • Located North of Maikal Range.
  • Bounded by Son river (North).
  • Rocks: Limestones/Sandstones (West), Granite (East).
  • Acts as Water Divide between Son (N) and Mahanadi (S).
  • Elevation: 150 - 1,200 m. Horizontal strata = no major disturbance.
  • Bhanrer & Kaimur near trough-axis.
North-East

Chotanagpur Plateau

  • NE projection (Jharkhand, N. Chhatisgarh, Purulia-WB).
  • Avg Elev: 700 m. Mainly Gondwana rocks.
  • Drainage: Radial pattern (Damodar, Subarnrekaha, N/S Koel, Barkar).
  • Damodar River flows W to E in rift valley = Gondwana Coal Fields.
  • Hazaribagh Plateau: North of Damodar, 600m, peneplain.
  • Ranchi Plateau: South of Damodar, 600m, Monadnocks (Ayers rock like).
  • Rajmahal Hills: NE edge, basaltic lava, N-S direction, 400m avg (567m peak).
Far-East

Meghalaya (Shillong) Plateau

  • Extension beyond Rajmahal, separated by Garo-Rajmahal Gap (down-faulting filled by Ganga/Brahmaputra).
  • Rocks: Archaean quartzites, shales, schists.
  • Slopes: Down to Brahmaputra valley (N) and Surma/Meghna (S).
  • Hills (W to E): Garo (900m), Khasi-Jaintia (1,500m), Mikir (700m).
  • Highest Point: Shillong (1,961 m).
South

Deccan Plateau

  • Area: 5 lakh sq km.
  • Bounds: Satpura/Vindhya/Mahadev/Maikal (N), Western/Eastern Ghats.
  • Elevation: 600 m Avg (Rises 1000m in S, dips 500m in N).
  • General slope is West to East.
  • Divided into smaller plateaus by rivers.
West-Deccan

Maharashtra Plateau

  • North part of Deccan Plateau.
  • Basaltic rocks of lava origin (Deccan Traps step-like topography).
  • Rolling plain due to weathering.
  • Valleys: Godavari, Bhima, Krishna (flanked by flat-topped hills).
  • Covered in Black Cotton Soil (Regur).
South

Karnataka (Mysore) Plateau

  • South of Maharashtra plateau; 600-900m elevation.
  • Highest Peak: Mulangiri (1,913 m) in Baba Budan Hills.
  • Two Parts:
    1. Malnad: Hill country, deep valleys, dense forests.
    2. Maidan: Rolling plain with low granite hills.
  • Tapers in south, merges with Niligiri hills.
South-East

Telangana Plateau

  • Made of Archaean gneisses.
  • Elevation: 500-600 m (South is higher than North).
  • Drained by: Godavari, Krishna, Penneru.
  • Divided into Ghats and Peneplains (vast featureless plains).
East-Central

Chhattisgarh Plain

  • The only plain worth the name in the Peninsular plateau.
  • Saucer-shaped depression drained by upper Mahanadi.
  • Between Maikala Range and Odisha hills.
  • Horizontal beds of limestone & shales.
  • Named after 36 forts of Haithaivanshi Rajputs.
  • Elevation: 250m (E) to 330m (W).
