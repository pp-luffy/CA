🥔 Biofortified Potato Varieties & Hidden Hunger

Background & Concept: Developed by ICAR and the Central Potato Research Institute (CPRI) to combat hidden hunger in India—a condition where individuals consume adequate calorie quantities (chapatis, rice) but suffer from severe micronutrient and protein deficiencies.

What is Biofortification? The process of breeding crops to increase their nutritional value through conventional plant breeding or biotechnology, enhancing vitamins, minerals, and protein content naturally.

Key Varieties Developed:

  • Kufri Neel (2018): Purple-skinned potato rich in antioxidants.
  • Kufri Manik (2021): Red potato rich in anthocyanins, zinc, and iron.
  • Kufri Jamuni: Purple-fleshed potato packed with Vitamin C, zinc, iron, anthocyanins, and carotenoids. Yields 32–35 tons/hectare in Northern plains.

⚠️ Prelims Trap Alert:

Do not confuse biofortification with genetic modification (GMO). Biofortification relies on conventional plant breeding techniques.

✍️ Mains Analytical Hook:

Biofortified crops serve as a sustainable tool for nutritional security, dietary diversity, and doubling farmers' income through value-added processed food products.

👩‍🌾 Mahila Kisan Diwas 2026

Overview: Celebrated annually on 15th October 2026 by the Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare (Department of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare) at Dr. Ambedkar International Center, New Delhi.

Theme 2026: "Role of women farmers in building developed agriculture and a developed India (Viksit Bharat)."

Key Objectives & Highlights:

  • Recognizing women's contribution in crop cultivation, animal husbandry, seed conservation, and rural livelihoods.
  • Honoring outstanding women farmers, with special emphasis on tribal regions.
  • Releasing the book titled "Women Farmers of India: Journey from Challenges to Success."
  • Empowering women through affordable credit, farm mechanization, skill development, and better market access.

✍️ Mains Analytical Hook:

The "feminization of agriculture" requires structural reforms ensuring land ownership rights, institutional credit access without collateral, and digital extension services for women farmers.

🌐 National Strategic Trade Command Centre (NSTCC)

Launched: 15th October 2026 by Union Minister of Commerce and Industry Piyush Goyal at Vanijya Bhawan, New Delhi. Developed under the Department of Commerce.

Core Mandate: A digital monitoring command center to track India's imports, exports, and international trade activities under one roof, strengthening trade intelligence and evidence-based policymaking.

Key Functions & Areas Covered:

  • Integrated Monitoring: Tracks trade performance, pending applications, and operational bottlenecks.
  • Core Schemes: Importer-Exporter Code (IEC), Advance Authorisation, Export Promotion Capital Goods (EPCG), Certificate of Origin (CoO).
  • SCOMET Oversight: Special Chemicals, Organisms, Materials, Equipment, and Technologies (dual-use items with civilian/military applications under FTDR Act 1992 and WMD Act 2005).
  • Platform Integration: Integrates Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT) systems and the Trade Connect e-platform.

✍️ Mains Analytical Hook:

NSTCC bridges administrative lag, aligns with UNSC Resolution 1540 on non-proliferation, and boosts India's export competitiveness in high-tech global value chains.

🚀 PM GatiShakti National Master Plan (5 Years)

Milestone: Completed 5 years in October 2026 (launched in October 2021 by the Prime Minister).

Developer & Technology: Developed by Bhaskaracharya National Institute for Space Applications and Geo-Informatics (BISAG-N) using geospatial technology.

Major Achievements & Features:

  • Integrated over 1,800 data layers spanning railways, highways, forests, and industrial zones.
  • Covers 58 central ministries and all 36 States/UTs.
  • Network Planning Group (NPG) evaluated 421 infrastructure projects worth over ₹20.22 lakh crore.
  • Significantly reduces logistical costs, eliminates work duplication across departments, and ensures last-mile multimodal connectivity.

✍️ Mains Analytical Hook:

GatiShakti transforms siloed infrastructure planning into a unified whole-of-government digital ecosystem, accelerating India's journey toward Viksit Bharat 2047.

🛡️ National Social Assistance Programme (NSAP)

Overview: Flagship social welfare scheme providing financial assistance to elderly, widows, persons with disabilities, and bereaved families. Nodal Ministry: Ministry of Rural Development. Launched initially on 15th August 1995.

Constitutional Link: Rooted in Article 41 of the Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP), directing the state to secure public assistance in cases of unemployment, old age, sickness, and disablement.

Recent Rebranding & Key Revisions:

  • New Name: Pradhan Mantri Samajik Sahayata Yojan.
  • Disability Eligibility: Minimum disability requirement reduced from 80% to 40%. Disability pension now eligible from birth.
  • Funding: 100% Central Assistance from the Government of India.
  • Sub-schemes: Old Age Pension, Widow Pension (Vidhwa), Divyangjan Pension, and National Family Benefit Scheme.

✍️ Mains Analytical Hook:

Expanding social safety nets through lowered disability thresholds directly aligns with India's obligations under the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act 2016.

🌊 India's Ramsar Wetlands (Total 103)

Recent Update: India's total Ramsar wetland tally reached 103 sites following the official announcement by the Union Environment Minister.

Two New Additions:

  • Sukhna Wetland: Located in Chandigarh.
  • Sauj Jheel: Located in Mainpuri district, Uttar Pradesh.

About Ramsar Convention: Adopted in 1971 in Ramsar, Iran (World Wetlands Day celebrated on 2nd February). It provides the framework for national action and international cooperation for the conservation and wise use of wetlands and their resources.

✍️ Mains Analytical Hook:

Wetland conservation acts as a natural buffer against climate change, ensuring groundwater recharge, flood attenuation, and carbon sequestration (Blue Carbon).

🐾 International Big Cat Alliance (IBCA)

Headquarters: Inaugurated at 55 Lodi Estate, New Delhi. IBCA is a treaty-based intergovernmental international organization headquartered in India.

Target Species (7 Big Cats): Lion, Tiger, Leopard, Snow Leopard, Cheetah, Jaguar, and Puma.

Key Highlights & Membership:

  • Membership: 29 member countries (including India), plus observer states.
  • Financial Commitment: India contributed ₹150 crore for 5 years (2023-24 to 2027-28).
  • PRIME Program: Specialized veterinary health training for wildlife doctors across 20 countries.
  • Collaborations: Works alongside CITES, UNFCCC, and ITEC capacity-building programs.

✍️ Mains Analytical Hook:

IBCA solidifies India's global leadership in biodiversity conservation, promoting South-South cooperation, habitat connectivity, and anti-poaching intelligence sharing.

🩺 Towards a TB-Free India & B-PaL Regimen

Causative Organism: Tuberculosis is an infectious bacterial disease caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis, primarily affecting the lungs.

Recent Performance: India's TB incidence declined by 21% between 2015 and 2024 (surpassing the global decline of 12%). Treatment coverage reached 92% (global average is 78%).

Key Interventions & Tech:

  • B-PaL Regimen: Shorter 6-month treatment course for drug-resistant TB (Bedaquiline, Pretomanid, Linezolid, Moxifloxacin).
  • Nikshay Poshan Yojana: ₹1,000 per month financial nutritional assistance for notified patients.
  • JOUSH Campaign: Joint Squad for Health launched to mobilize youth for TB awareness.
  • Technology: AI-enabled portable X-rays and AI chatbot 'Kushi' for screening.

✍️ Mains Analytical Hook:

Eliminating TB ahead of the global Sustainable Development Goal (SDG 3 target by 2030) requires addressing nutritional poverty, expanding molecular diagnostics, and community-led health governance.
