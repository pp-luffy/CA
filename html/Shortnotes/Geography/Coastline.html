Geography

Indian Coastline

  • Total length: 7516.6 Km (6100 km mainland + 1197 km islands)
  • Touches 13 States and UTs.
  • Formation: Result of faulting of Gondwanaland during the Cretaceous period (Continental Drift).
  • Harbours: Straight & regular coast. Lacks many natural harbours (unlike indented European coastlines).
  • The Bay of Bengal & Arabian Sea formed during the Cretaceous or early Tertiary period after Gondwanaland disintegration.
Geology

Emergence & Submergence

  • Emergence: Formed by uplift of land or lowering of sea level. Features: Bars, spits, lagoons, salt marshes, beaches, sea cliffs, arches.
  • Submergence: Exact opposite. Formed by sinking land/rising sea.
  • East Coast (Tamil Nadu): Coast of Emergence.
  • West Coast: Mixed. Northern part is submerged (due to faulting). Southern (Kerala) is emergent.
  • Coramandal Coast (TN): Emergence
  • Malabar Coast (Kerala): Emergence
  • Konkan Coast (MH & Goa): Submergence
East Coast

East Coast & Eastern Plains

  • Lies between Eastern Ghats & Bay of Bengal.
  • Extends from Subarnarekha river / Ganga delta to Kanniyakumari.
  • Extensive plains: Average width of 120 km.
  • Marked by large river deltas: Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Cauvery.
  • Features: Chilka lake, Pulicat lake (lagoon).
  • Northern region (Mahanadi to Krishna) = Northern Circars.
  • Southern region (Krishna to Cauvery) = Carnatic.
East Coast

Utkal, Andhra & TN Plains

  • Utkal Plain (Odisha): Mahanadi delta. Features Chilka Lake (biggest in country, 780 sq km winter - 1144 sq km monsoon). Low hills to the south.
  • Andhra Plain: South of Utkal to Pulicat Lake (barred by Sriharikota Island - ISRO). Godavari & Krishna deltas merged here.
  • Delta advanced 35 km into sea recently -> Kolleru Lake is now far inland! Lacks good harbours except Vishakhapatnam & Machilipatnam.
  • Tamil Nadu Plain: Pulicat to KK (675 km long). Cauvery delta is 130 km wide ("granary of South India").
  • Coramandal Coast / Payan Ghat: False Divi Point (AP/Krishna Delta) to Kanyakumari.
West Coast

West Coast & Western Plains

  • Extends from Rann of Kachchh / Gulf of Cambay to Cape Comorin (KK).
  • Narrow plains: Average width of 65 km.
  • Made of alluvium from short Western Ghats streams.
  • Dotted with coves (small bays), creeks, and estuaries (Narmada and Tapi are major).
  • Regional divisions: Konkan (MH & Goa), Karnataka, Kerala / Malabar.
West Coast

Gujarat, Kutch & Kathiawar

  • Kutch & Kathiawar: Extension of Peninsular plateau (Kathiawar = Deccan Lava, Kutch = Tertiary rocks), now levelled down.
  • Kutch Peninsula: Former island filled by Indus River sediment. Now arid/semi-arid.
  • Rann of Kutch: Great Rann (salt-soaked, North) & Little Rann (South/SE).
  • Kathiawar: Mandav Hills (radial drainage). Mt. Girnar (1117m) highest point, volcanic. Gir Range has dense forests, home of Gir lion.
  • Gujarat Plain: East of Kachchh. Rivers: Narmada, Tapi, Mahi, Sabarmati. East is fertile; coast has windblown loess (sand heaps).
West Coast

Konkan & Karnataka Plains

  • Konkan Plain: Daman to Goa (50-80 km wide). Marine erosion features: cliffs, shoals, reefs.
  • Thane Creek (Mumbai) provides excellent natural harbour.
  • Karnataka Coastal Plain: Goa to Mangalore (30-50 km wide, max 70 km). Marked marine topography.
  • Steep streams make waterfalls: Sharavati river creates Gersoppa (Jog) Falls (271m). *(Trivia: Angel falls 979m (highest), Tugela 948m (second)).*
West Coast

Kerala / Malabar Plain

  • Mangalore to Kanniyakumari. Wider than Karnataka plain, low-lying.
  • Famous for lakes, lagoons, spits, and backwaters (Kayals) - shallow lagoons parallel to the coastline.
  • Vembanad Lake: Largest backwater, 75 km long and 5-10 km wide.
  • Gives rise to a 55 km long spit.
Economy

Economic Significance

  • Agriculture: Fertile soils (Rice is the main crop). Coconut trees grow abundantly along the coast.
  • Trade: Coast dotted with big/small ports for commerce. Fishing is a major occupation.
  • Minerals & Energy: Sedimentary rocks contain mineral oil (KG Basin).
  • Nuclear Power: Kerala coast sands contain huge deposits of MONAZITE.
  • Salt: Low-lying areas of Gujarat produce large amounts of salt.
  • Tourism: Kerala backwaters and Goa beaches are major international tourist destinations.
