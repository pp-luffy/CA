⚡ Core Exam Revision Grid ⚡

Himalayan Rivers Perennial (snow & rain). Create V-shaped valleys (youth), ox-bow lakes & deltas (plains). Key: Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra.
Peninsular Rivers Non-perennial/seasonal. Fixed courses, no meanders. Older than Himalayan. Western Ghats acts as the main water divide.
West Flowing Drainages Narmada (largest) & Tapi. Flow in rift valleys between Vindhya & Satpura. Amarkantak & Betul origins.
East Flowing Drainages Mahanadi, Godavari (Dakshin Ganga), Krishna, Kaveri. Drain into Bay of Bengal forming major deltas.
Drainage Patterns Dendritic (Tree), Trellis (Folds/90°), Parallel (Steep slope), Rectangular (Faults/Joints), Radial (Dome/Volcano), Centripetal (Depression/Lake).
Tectonic Forces Folding = Flexible crust (Mountains, Anticlines & Synclines). Faulting = Brittle crust breaks (Earthquakes & Rift Valleys).
💦 Channels & Networks

Drainage & Drainage Pattern

Drainage: Flow of water through well-defined channels. The network of these channels is called a drainage system.

Drainage Pattern: The spatial system of surface water flow.

  • Depends heavily on: slope of land, geological structure, volume of water, and velocity.
Fundamentals
🌳 Tree Roots Branching

Dendritic Pattern

  • Most common form; resembles branching tree roots.
  • Develops where channel strictly follows the terrain slope.
  • Sub-surface rock has no particular structure; eroded equally in all directions.
  • Tributaries join main stream at acute angles (<90°).
  • Example: Northern plains rivers (Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra).
Geo-Pattern
⏸️ Elongated Slopes

Parallel Pattern

  • Develops in regions of parallel, elongated landforms.
  • Requires a pronounced surface slope.
  • Tributary streams stretch out in a parallel-like fashion along the slope.
  • Example: Rivers originating in Western Ghats (Godavari, Kaveri, Krishna, Tungabhadra).
Geo-Pattern
📐 Folded Topography (90°)

Trellis Pattern

  • Develops in folded topography where hard & soft rocks alternate parallel to each other.
  • Down-turned folds (synclines) form valleys for the main channel.
  • Primary tributaries flow parallel; secondary join them at right angles (90°).
  • Example: Upper part of Himalayan region.
Geo-Pattern
🪨 Faulted & Jointed Terrain

Rectangular Pattern

  • Found in regions that have undergone geological faulting.
  • Develops on strongly joined, brittle rocky terrain.
  • Streams follow paths of least resistance (weakest exposed rock).
  • Tributary streams make sharp bends, entering main stream at high angles.
  • Example: Vindhya mountain range streams (Chambal, Betwa, Ken).
Geo-Pattern
⚠️ Compression Forces

Folding and Faulting

Occurs when Earth's crust is pushed together via compression forces.

  • Folding: Crust bends away from a flat surface (flexible material). Forms mountains.
    • Bend upward = Anticline
    • Bend downward = Syncline
  • Faulting: Crust completely breaks and slides past each other (brittle material). Causes earthquakes.
Tectonics
🌋 Outward from Center

Radial Pattern

  • Develops around a central elevated point.
  • Common to conically shaped features such as volcanoes or domes.
  • Rivers originate from the peak and flow outward in all directions.
  • Example: Rivers from Amarkantak range (Narmada and Son - tributary of Ganga).
Geo-Pattern
🎯 Inward to Depression

Centripetal Pattern

  • Exact opposite of radial; streams flow inward toward a central depression.
  • During wet portions of the year, streams feed ephemeral lakes.
  • Lakes evaporate away during dry periods.
  • Salt dissolved in water precipitates, creating salt flats.
  • Example: Loktak lake in Manipur.
Geo-Pattern
🏔️ Melting Snow & Rain

Himalayan Drainage System

  • Rivers fed by both melting snow and precipitation; highly perennial.
  • Mountain course (Youth): Form V-shaped valleys, rapids, and steep waterfalls.
  • Plains course (Mature/Old): Form depositional features like flat valleys, ox-bow lakes, flood plains, braided channels, and massive deltas near the mouth.
Himalayan
🌍 Singi Khamban

Indus River System

  • One of the largest river basins of the world. Westernmost Himalayan river, known as Sindhu.
  • Origin: Glacier near Bokhar Chu in Tibetan Kailash range. Known in Tibet as 'Singi Khamban' (Lion’s mouth).
  • Flows in India exclusively through Leh district (Ladakh UT).
  • Key Tributaries: Sutlej, Ravi, Jhelum, Chenab (largest tributary), Beas.
Himalayan
🌊 Devprayag Confluence

Ganga River System

  • Origin: Gangotri glacier near Gaumukh (3,900m, Uttarakhand) as Bhagirathi.
  • Devprayag: Bhagirathi meets Alaknanda = Ganga. Enters plains at Haridwar.
  • Flows via UK, UP, Bihar, WB into Bay of Bengal near Sagar Island.
  • Right Bank Tribs: Son, Yamuna (westernmost, longest, source: Yamunotri).
  • Left Bank Tribs: Ramganga, Gomati, Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi, Mahananda.
Himalayan
🌀 The Purifier

Brahmaputra River System

  • Origin: Chemayungdung glacier (Kailash range) near Mansarovar lake.
  • Tibet Name: Tsangpo (meaning 'the purifier').
  • Emerges from foothills as Siang/Dihang; enters India west of Sadiya (Arunachal Pradesh).
  • Left Bank Tribs: Dibang/Sikang, Lohit, Burhi Dihing, Dhansari.
  • Right Bank Tribs: Subansiri, Kameng, Manas, Sankosh.
  • Merges with river Padma in Bangladesh.
Himalayan
🕰️ Ancient Courses

Peninsular Drainage System

  • Geologically older than the Himalayan drainage system.
  • Characterised by fixed courses, absence of meanders, and nonperennial (seasonal) flow.
  • Primary Water Divide: Western Ghats running close to west coast.
  • Most major rivers (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri) flow West to East.
  • Exceptions: Narmada and Tapi flow Westward.
Peninsular
⬅️ West Flowing (Rift)

Narmada River

  • Largest west flowing river of the peninsular region.
  • Flows exclusively through a rift valley between Vindhya (north) and Satpura Range (south).
  • Origin: Maikala range near Amarkantak (MP).
  • Tributaries: Hiran, Orsang, Barna, Kolar.
  • Basin spans MP, Maharashtra, and Gujarat. Hosts the Sardar Sarovar Project.
Peninsular
⬅️ Parallel Rift

Tapi River

  • Another crucial westward flowing river.
  • Origin: Betul district of Madhya Pradesh in the Satpura ranges.
  • Flows in a rift valley strictly parallel to the Narmada but is much shorter in length.
  • Basin comprehensively covers parts of MP, Gujarat, and Maharashtra.
Peninsular
➡️ East to Bay of Bengal

Mahanadi River

  • Origin: Rises in Raipur district (Chhattisgarh), runs through Odisha to discharge in Bay of Bengal.
  • Basin Share: 53% lies in MP & Chhattisgarh, 47% lies in Odisha.
  • Tributaries: Seonath, Hasdeo, Mand, Ib, Jonking, and Tel rivers.
  • Geographically bounded by Central India hills (N), Eastern Ghats (S, E), and Maikala range (W).
Peninsular
👑 Dakshin Ganga

Godavari River

  • The largest Peninsular river system, proudly called "Dakshin Ganga".
  • Origin: Rises in Nasik district of Maharashtra.
  • Tributary network runs through Maharashtra, MP, Chhattisgarh, Odisha, and AP.
  • Principal Tributaries: Penganga, Indravati, Pranhita, and Manjra.
Peninsular
🌊 2nd Largest East-Flowing

Krishna River

  • The second largest east flowing Peninsular river.
  • Origin: Rises near Mahabaleshwar in Sahyadri.
  • Major Tributaries: Koyna, Tungbhadra, and Bhima.
  • Flows through four major states: Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, and Andhra Pradesh before Bay of Bengal.
Peninsular
🛕 Sacred Southern Waters

Kaveri River

  • Origin: Rises in Brahmagiri hills of Kodagu district (Karnataka).
  • Considered a highly sacred river of southern India.
  • Important Tributaries: Arkavathi, Hemavathi, Bhavani, Kabini, and Amravati.
  • Flows southeasterly through Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, draining via Pondicherry.
