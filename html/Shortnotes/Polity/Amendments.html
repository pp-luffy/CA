1951
J. Nehru (INC)

1st Amendment

💡 Why: Early court rulings (Champakam Dorairajan, Romesh Thappar) struck down caste quotas, zamindari abolition, and speech curbs right after the Constitution began.
  • Added the 9th Schedule (via Art 31B) and Article 31A to shield land reform and zamindari abolition laws from judicial review.
  • Inserted Article 15(4) empowering the state to make special provisions for socially and educationally backward classes.
  • Added 3 new grounds of reasonable restrictions on Freedom of Speech (Art 19(2)): Public Order, Friendly Relations with Foreign States, and Incitement to an Offence.
9th Schedule Art 15(4) Speech Restrictions
1955
J. Nehru (INC)

4th Amendment

💡 Why: SC in Bela Banerjee (1954) ruled that "compensation" for acquired land must equal full market value, threatening to bankrupt land reform and nationalisation drives.
  • Made the adequacy of compensation paid for compulsory acquisition of private property non-justiciable (courts could not question the amount).
  • Authorized the State to nationalize any trade or business (State monopolies immune from Art 19(1)(g) challenges).
  • Expanded the scope of Article 31A and added more acts to the 9th Schedule.
Property Compensation State Monopolies
1960
J. Nehru (INC)

9th Amendment

💡 Why: When Nehru agreed to transfer Berubari Union to Pakistan, the SC advised that Parliament cannot cede Indian territory by ordinary law under Art 3.
  • Facilitated the cession of Indian territory (Berubari Union in West Bengal) to Pakistan under the 1958 Indo-Pak Agreement.
  • Confirmed the Berubari Union Case (1960) principle that ceding territory to a foreign state requires a Constitutional Amendment under Article 368.
Berubari Union Art 368 Mandate
1963
J. Nehru (INC)

16th Amendment

💡 Why: After the 1962 China war and rising regional secessionist demands (e.g., Dravida Nadu), the National Integration Council pushed an "Anti-Secession" amendment.
  • Added "sovereignty and integrity of India" as a new ground of reasonable restriction under Articles 19(2), 19(3), and 19(4) (speech, assembly, and associations).
  • Amended the Third Schedule to include upholding the "sovereignty and integrity of India" in the oaths of election candidates, MPs/MLAs, Ministers, SC/HC Judges, and the CAG.
Art 19(2)(3)(4) 3rd Schedule Oath
1964
Lal Bahadur Shastri (INC)

17th Amendment

💡 Why: Kerala and Madras High Courts struck down state land-ceiling laws on ryotwari estates, prompting Parliament to shield 44 more land Acts.
  • Prohibited the acquisition of land under personal cultivation unless compensation at full market value was paid.
  • Inserted 44 more land reform acts into the 9th Schedule — this directly triggered the landmark Golaknath Case (1967).
9th Schedule (+44 Acts) Trigger for Golaknath
1971
Indira Gandhi (INC)

24th Amendment

💡 Why: Enacted to overturn Golaknath (1967), where the SC had ruled that Parliament had no power to amend or curtail Fundamental Rights.
  • Affirmed Parliament's power to amend any part of the Constitution, including Fundamental Rights (amended Art 13 and Art 368).
  • Made it compulsory for the President to give assent to a Constitutional Amendment Bill (no veto/return power).
Amend Fundamental Rights Mandatory Pres Assent
1971
Indira Gandhi (INC)

25th Amendment

💡 Why: Passed after the SC struck down Bank Nationalisation in R.C. Cooper (1970) for offering illusory compensation instead of fair value.
  • Curtailed the Right to Property: replaced the word "compensation" with "amount" in Art 31(2) to bypass judicial scrutiny of market value.
  • Inserted Article 31C, providing that any law giving effect to socialist DPSPs under Article 39(b) and 39(c) cannot be declared void for violating Articles 14, 19, or 31.
  • Also barred judicial review of such declarations — this second part was struck down in Kesavananda Bharati (1973) as violating the Basic Structure.
Article 31C DPSP 39(b)&(c) > FR Property Curtailed
1973
Indira Gandhi (INC)

31st Amendment

💡 Why: Rapid population growth revealed in the 1971 Census demanded an increase in Lok Sabha seats to keep representation proportional across states.
  • Increased the number of Lok Sabha seats from 525 to 545 (following the 1971 Census).
  • Reduced the representation of Union Territories in the Lok Sabha from 25 to 20.
Lok Sabha: 525 → 545
1975
Indira Gandhi (INC)

38th Amendment

💡 Why: Passed right after the 1975 National Emergency was declared to immunise Emergency proclamations and Ordinances from being challenged in courts.
  • Made the declaration of Emergency by the President non-justiciable.
  • Made the promulgation of Ordinances by the President, Governors, and UT Administrators non-justiciable.
  • Empowered the President to declare different proclamations of National Emergency on different grounds simultaneously. (Judicial review bar was later removed by the 44th Amendment.)
Emergency Era Non-Justiciable Ordinances
1975
Indira Gandhi (INC)

39th Amendment

💡 Why: Rushed through in days after the Allahabad High Court invalidated Indira Gandhi's 1971 Rae Bareli election in Raj Narain's petition.
  • Placed election disputes relating to the President, Vice-President, Prime Minister, and Speaker of Lok Sabha beyond the jurisdiction of the judiciary (to be decided by a body determined by Parliament).
  • Struck down by the Supreme Court in Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975) for violating free & fair elections and judicial review (Basic Structure).
Struck Down (Basic Structure) PM Election Dispute
1976
Indira Gandhi (INC)

42nd Amendment (Mini-Constitution)

💡 Why: Peak-Emergency overhaul on the Swaran Singh Committee's advice to cement Parliamentary supremacy over the judiciary and centralise power.
  • Added "Socialist, Secular, and Integrity" to the Preamble.
  • Added Fundamental Duties (Part IVA, Article 51A) and 3 new DPSPs: Art 39A (free legal aid), Art 43A (workers in management), Art 48A (environment & wildlife) + modified Art 39(f).
  • Made the President bound by Cabinet advice (Art 74). Added Tribunals (Part XIV-A: Art 323A & 323B).
  • Shifted 5 subjects from State to Concurrent List: Education, Forests, Protection of Wild Animals & Birds, Weights & Measures, and Administration of Justice.
  • Extended Art 31C to protect all DPSPs over Art 14 & 19 (later struck down in Minerva Mills, 1980).
Emergency Era Mini-Constitution Fundamental Duties 5 Concurrent Subjects
1977
Morarji Desai (Janata)

43rd Amendment

💡 Why: The newly elected Janata government's urgent first step to restore judicial review and dismantle Emergency-era curbs on courts.
  • Restored the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court and High Courts regarding judicial review and issuing writs.
  • Repealed Article 31D, depriving Parliament of special powers to enact laws dealing with "anti-national activities".
Judicial Review Restored Art 31D Repealed
1978
Morarji Desai (Janata)

44th Amendment

💡 Why: Built permanent constitutional guardrails against future Emergency abuse and ended decades of FR-based Right to Property litigation.
  • Removed Right to Property from Fundamental Rights (repealed Art 19(1)(f) & Art 31) and made it a legal/constitutional right under Article 300A.
  • Replaced "internal disturbance" with "armed rebellion" as a ground for National Emergency; required written recommendation of the Cabinet.
  • Guaranteed that Articles 20 and 21 cannot be suspended even during a National Emergency.
  • Empowered the President to send back Cabinet advice once for reconsideration. Restored Lok Sabha/Assembly term from 6 years back to 5 years.
Art 300A (Property) Armed Rebellion Art 20 & 21 Inviolable
1985
Rajiv Gandhi (INC)

52nd Amendment

💡 Why: Rampant political horse-trading ("Aaya Ram, Gaya Ram" culture) repeatedly toppled elected state governments throughout the 1960s–80s.
  • Introduced the Anti-Defection Law by adding the 10th Schedule to disqualify MPs and MLAs on grounds of defection.
  • Amended Articles 101, 102, 190, and 191. Presiding Officer's decision is subject to judicial review (Kihoto Hollohan, 1992).
Anti-Defection (10th Sch)
1989
Rajiv Gandhi (INC)

61st Amendment

💡 Why: Rajiv Gandhi pushed to bring politically aware youth into mainstream democracy and align India with global voting-age standards.
  • Reduced the voting age from 21 years to 18 years for Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assembly elections by amending Article 326.
Voting Age 18 (Art 326)
1992
P.V. Narasimha Rao (INC)

73rd & 74th Amendments

💡 Why: Local bodies suffered from irregular elections and zero financial autonomy, leading the L.M. Singhvi Committee to urge constitutional backing for grassroots democracy.
  • 73rd Amendment: Constitutional status to Panchayati Raj Institutions — added Part IX (Art 243–243O) and 11th Schedule (29 functional items).
  • 74th Amendment: Constitutional status to Urban Local Bodies / Municipalities — added Part IXA (Art 243P–243ZG) and 12th Schedule (18 functional items).
Panchayats (11th Sch) Municipalities (12th Sch)
1995
P.V. Narasimha Rao (INC)

77th Amendment

💡 Why: Enacted to bypass the SC's Indra Sawhney (1992) verdict, which had limited quotas strictly to initial appointments and barred them in promotions.
  • Inserted Article 16(4A) empowering the State to provide reservation in promotions in government jobs for SCs and STs if inadequately represented.
Art 16(4A) Quota in Promotions
2000
A.B. Vajpayee (NDA)

81st Amendment

💡 Why: Indra Sawhney capped total annual quotas (including carried-forward vacancies) at 50%, leaving unfilled SC/ST backlog posts perpetually vacant.
  • Inserted Article 16(4B) to allow unfilled reserved backlog vacancies to be carried forward as a separate class in subsequent years.
  • Ended the 50% ceiling limit on reservation for such backlog vacancies.
Art 16(4B) Backlog Exempt from 50% Cap
2001
A.B. Vajpayee (NDA)

84th Amendment

💡 Why: Southern states controlled population growth faster than northern states; freezing seats till 2026 prevented them from being penalised with fewer Lok Sabha seats.
  • Extended the freeze on readjustment of seats in the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies for another 25 years — until 2026 (based on the 1971 Census) to incentivize population control.
Seat Freeze till 2026 Delimitation
2001
A.B. Vajpayee (NDA)

85th Amendment

💡 Why: SC's Virpal Singh and Ajit Singh verdicts applied the "catch-up rule", letting general candidates regain seniority over promoted SC/ST juniors.
  • Amended Article 16(4A) to grant consequential seniority in promotions for SC and ST government servants (with retrospective effect from June 1995).
  • Constitutional validity upheld in the M. Nagaraj Case (2006) subject to backwardness, inadequacy, and administrative efficiency checks.
Consequential Seniority Art 16(4A)
2002
A.B. Vajpayee (NDA)

86th Amendment

💡 Why: After the SC in Unni Krishnan (1993) read elementary education into Article 21, Parliament elevated it to an explicit, enforceable Fundamental Right.
  • Inserted Article 21A making free & compulsory Right to Education (6–14 years) a Fundamental Right.
  • Changed Article 45 (DPSP) to early childhood care & education for children below 6 years.
  • Added the 11th Fundamental Duty under Article 51A(k) for parents/guardians to provide education opportunities.
Art 21A (RTE) Art 51A(k) Art 45 Updated
2003
A.B. Vajpayee (NDA)

91st Amendment

💡 Why: Era of fractured coalitions spawned bloated "jumbo cabinets" and frequent 1/3rd party splits engineered purely to grab ministerial berths.
  • Capped the size of the Council of Ministers (including PM/CM) at 15% of the total strength of the Lok Sabha / State Assembly (minimum 12 ministers for States).
  • Strengthened the Anti-Defection Law: omitted the 1/3rd split exemption (only a 2/3rd merger is now protected) and barred disqualified legislators from holding ministerial posts.
15% CoM Cap 1/3rd Split Omitted
2005
Manmohan Singh (UPA)

93rd Amendment

💡 Why: Enacted to overturn the SC's P.A. Inamdar (2005) judgment, which had barred the State from imposing reservation quotas in private unaided colleges.
  • Inserted Article 15(5) empowering the State to make special provisions (reservations) for socially & educationally backward classes, SCs, and STs in private educational institutions (aided or unaided), except minority institutions.
Art 15(5) Private Inst. Quota
2011
Manmohan Singh (UPA)

97th Amendment

💡 Why: Widespread political interference, superseded boards, and delayed elections crippled rural cooperatives, prompting constitutional autonomy.
  • Granted constitutional status to Co-operative Societies: made forming co-operatives a Fundamental Right under Art 19(1)(c), added DPSP Art 43B, and inserted Part IXB.
  • Note: In 2021, the SC struck down Part IXB provisions relating to State co-operatives for lacking state ratification (retained for multi-state co-operatives).
Part IXB Art 19(1)(c) & 43B
2014
Narendra Modi (NDA)

99th Amendment

💡 Why: Growing criticism that the judicial Collegium was opaque and "judges appointing judges" pushed Parliament to create a joint appointment commission.
  • Established the National Judicial Appointments Commission (NJAC) to replace the Collegium system for appointing SC/HC judges.
  • Struck down as unconstitutional by the Supreme Court in the Fourth Judges Case (2015) for violating judicial independence (Basic Structure).
Struck Down (2015) NJAC vs Collegium
2015
Narendra Modi (NDA)

100th Amendment

💡 Why: Resolved decades-old border enclaves (chitmahals) that left ~50,000 people virtually stateless along the India–Bangladesh border.
  • Operationalized the India–Bangladesh Land Boundary Agreement (LBA) of 1974 and its 2011 Protocol, enabling the exchange of enclaves and transfer of territories across Assam, West Bengal, Meghalaya, and Tripura.
LBA with Bangladesh
2016
Narendra Modi (NDA)

101st Amendment

💡 Why: India's tangled web of indirect taxes (VAT, Excise, Service Tax) caused cascading "tax on tax" and choked interstate commerce.
  • Introduced the Goods and Services Tax (GST) — "One Nation, One Tax" — conferring concurrent taxing powers on Parliament and State Legislatures (Art 246A).
  • Created the constitutional GST Council under Article 279A chaired by the Union Finance Minister.
GST (Art 246A) GST Council (Art 279A)
2018
Narendra Modi (NDA)

102nd Amendment

💡 Why: NCBC was only a statutory body (1993 Act) with limited grievance-redressal teeth compared to constitutional bodies like NCSC and NCST.
  • Conferred constitutional status on the National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC) by inserting Article 338B.
  • Inserted Article 342A empowering the President to notify Socially and Educationally Backward Classes (SEBCs).
NCBC (Art 338B) Art 342A
2019
Narendra Modi (NDA)

103rd Amendment

💡 Why: Addressed rising demands for economic-criteria affirmative action for poor citizens outside existing SC/ST/OBC reservation categories.
  • Introduced a 10% reservation for Economically Weaker Sections (EWS) in educational institutions and public employment by inserting Articles 15(6) and 16(6).
  • Upheld by a 3:2 Supreme Court bench in Janhit Abhiyan v. Union of India (2022).
10% EWS Quota Art 15(6) & 16(6)
2020
Narendra Modi (NDA)

104th Amendment

💡 Why: SC/ST legislative quotas were expiring in January 2020, while the tiny Anglo-Indian community was deemed well-integrated without needing reserved nominations.
  • Extended the reservation of seats for SCs and STs in the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies for another 10 years (until 2030) under Art 334.
  • Abolished the nomination of Anglo-Indian members (2 in Lok Sabha, 1 in State Assemblies).
SC/ST Quota till 2030 Anglo-Indian Seats Removed
2021
Narendra Modi (NDA)

105th Amendment

💡 Why: SC's Maratha Quota (2021) verdict interpreted the 102nd Amendment as stripping States of power to maintain their own OBC lists.
  • Amended Article 342A to restore the power of State governments and UTs to independently identify and maintain their own SEBC/OBC lists.
State OBC Lists Restored Art 342A Clarified
2023
Narendra Modi (NDA)

106th Amendment

💡 Why: Women's share in Lok Sabha stayed stuck near ~15% for decades, finally pushing through a 27-year-pending bill for legislative gender parity.
  • Enacted as the Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam.
  • Mandates 33% reservation for women in the Lok Sabha (Art 330A), State Legislative Assemblies (Art 332A), and the NCT of Delhi Assembly (Art 239AA) for 15 years — to come into effect after the next Census and Delimitation.
33% Women's Quota Nari Shakti Vandan
