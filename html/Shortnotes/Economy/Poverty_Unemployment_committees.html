📌 Part 1: Poverty & Unemployment (Core Concepts)

Poverty Basics

1. Types & Measurement of Poverty

  • Absolute Poverty: Inability to meet basic minimum needs (food, shelter, health). Used in developing nations (India).
  • Relative Poverty: Income compared to society's average (inequality). Used in developed nations.
  • Head Count Ratio (HCR): % of population living below the poverty line.
  • Poverty Gap Index: Measures depth/intensity of poverty (distance from poverty line).
  • Lorenz Curve: Graphical measure of income inequality (45° line = perfect equality).
  • Gini Coefficient: Numerical value from Lorenz curve — 0 = Perfect Equality, 1 = Perfect Inequality.
  • PURA Model (OPSC PYQ): Providing Urban Amenities to Rural Areas — proposed by Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam to eradicate rural poverty via 4 connectivities (Physical, Electronic, Knowledge, Economic).
Chronology

2. Poverty Estimation Committees

  • Dadabhai Naoroji (1901): 1st ever estimate (Poverty & Un-British Rule in India) — "Jail Cost of Living".
  • Dandekar & Rath (1971): 1st post-independence scientific estimate (2250 kcal norm).
  • Alagh Committee (1979): Calorie-based poverty line — 2400 kcal (Rural) & 2100 kcal (Urban).
  • Lakdawala Committee (1993): Retained Alagh calorie norm; introduced state-specific poverty lines (CPI-AL rural, CPI-IW urban).
  • Tendulkar Committee (2009):
    • Dropped calorie norm; shifted to Mixed Reference Period (MRP); added health & education.
    • Line (2011–12): ₹27/day (Rural), ₹33/day (Urban) → 21.9% poor.
  • Rangarajan Committee (2014): Used MMRP + protein/fat norms. Line: ₹32/day (Rural), ₹47/day (Urban) → 29.5% poor (not officially adopted).
  • NITI Aayog (Current — OPSC PYQ): Official poverty assessment body. Uses National MPI (3 dimensions: Health, Education, Standard of Living; 12 indicators — adds Maternal Health & Bank Account to Global MPI's 10).
Unemployment

3. Types of Unemployment

  • Disguised Unemployment: More people employed than required; Marginal Productivity = 0. Seen in Indian agriculture.
  • Seasonal Unemployment: Work available only during specific seasons (sowing/harvesting, tourism).
  • Structural Unemployment: Long-term mismatch between workers' skills and job demand (automation, industrial shift).
  • Frictional Unemployment: Short-term/temporary gap while switching jobs. Exists even at full employment.
  • Cyclical Unemployment: Caused by economic recessions / downturns in business cycle (demand deficiency).
  • Underemployment: Working below skill level or part-time when seeking full-time work.
  • Technological Unemployment: Job loss directly due to AI / machinery replacing labour.
🔥 Natural Rate of Unemployment = Frictional + Structural (Cyclical = 0).
NSO & Curves

4. Unemployment Measurement & Curves

  • Nodal Agency: NSO (MoSPI) conducts PLFS (Periodic Labour Force Survey).
  • 3 NSO Reference Measures:
    • Usual Status (US): 365 days — captures chronic/open unemployment.
    • Current Weekly Status (CWS): 7 days — unemployed if not worked even 1 hour in the week.
    • Current Daily Status (CDS): Person-day basis — most comprehensive/sensitive measure (captures underemployment).
  • Key Ratios:
    • LFPR = (Employed + Unemployed) / Total Pop (15+ yrs).
    • Unemployment Rate (UR) = Unemployed / Labour Force.
  • Must-Know Curves:
    • Phillips Curve: Inverse relationship between Inflation & Unemployment (short-run).
    • Okun's Law: Inverse relationship between GDP growth & Unemployment.
